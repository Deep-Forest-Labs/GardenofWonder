Fall and Spring get a face — and Holly's eyes, revisited

Three candidates for each season, drawn to Poppy's construction and judged beside Poppy and Holly. The ruling (2026-09-27): simple like Poppy — one face, one petal shape, one palette — under Holly's kit, and a silhouette that is neither Poppy's round rosette nor Holly's crowned rose. Eyes read first, cute and clear at 46px; mischief is a tilt on a readable eye, never a squint. Gender is open. The four should read as one family — Gran's tin held one seed for each season. 0 · The natures, written before the drawing

Holly's nature comes from her season's rule — Winter is the night shift, so she is the one who keeps the garden while you sleep. The same move, once per season:

FALLThe rule: hours, and the bed pays together. Planted in the morning, popped at dinner; the windfall only lands when all eight are ripe at once.
So Fall's hero is the one who waits until everyone is in, and then calls them to the table. Nothing gets picked early on their watch.
SPRINGThe rule: the long game. The nursery, Prisms spent in ceremony, breeding one day — the garden about the other three.
So Spring's hero plants for a year they will not see — patient, a little ceremonious, the keeper of what hasn't come up yet.

1 · The lineup — all eight, in the garden's centre cell

The centre cell at its real size on a 390-wide phone (a little smaller on a narrower screen), the flower at the talking flower's own 118%, standing on the planter's material. Same outline weight, same face radius, same stem and leaves — the seasons differ in silhouette and palette.

One eye recipe for the family, and it is Poppy's own — the white eye, the ink pupil, one shine, her blink lid. Each new hero varies only tilt, a resting lid, pupil size and where it looks. It works for the new six because none of them has a pale face (Holly's lesson: a white eye on porcelain reads as spectacles). Cheeks are Poppy's pink on all six — the family blush; Holly still does not blush.

A male read is carried by one stroke above each eye and a wider mouth — Poppy's face, with no brow, is the family's default and reads as a girl. A female read is carried by a single lash flick at the outer corner (Holly's own, one instead of two) and a head tilt. No bows, no moustaches, no lashes doing the eye's work.

2 · GATE — the faces at 46px, inside the picker's mask

The avatar picker's circle, its veil over each hero's tint, its 3px lip. Poppy is drawn as the picker draws her today (the whole plant at 84%); every other hero is fitted — the whole head inside the disc — which is Holly's rule since her crown was clipped by this mask (doc 46).

All six survive the mask fitted, and none loses its identity to it. The cost is face size, and the wide shapes pay it: the Mop fits at 0.74 and the Dinnerplate at 0.72, against Holly's 0.78 — a face about a sixth smaller than the Cup's or the Trumpet's (0.9). The picker cell measured in docs/screens is ~62px on a 390 phone; 46px is doc 46's floor, drawn here because it is the harder test.

Should Poppy be fitted too? She is the only face in the row drawn as a whole plant, so at 46px her face is the smallest of the eight. Fitting her is one line in ui-menu.js.

3 · GATE — the black silhouette

Filled plain black, each hero must read as itself and as nobody else in the game. Same fitted heads, 46px and 120px. The caption under each is the one thing that makes it not-Poppy and not-Holly.

4 · Holly's eyes — her head as built, beside three treatments

Everything else about her is identical and untouched: crown, petals, porcelain, mark, mouth. Only the eyes change. Her current eye is one solid ink almond with two lashes — drawn that way because Poppy's white eye read as spectacles on porcelain (flora.js, "A porcelain face cannot wear the Talking Flower's eyes").

The built eye is drawn the wrong way round, and that is most of the problem. The spike that designed her described "angled down toward the nose, with two short lashes off the outer tip". In hollyEye() the lash path runs to +x on the unmirrored left eye — the inner side — so the almond's point and both lashes sit at the nose, the eyes rise toward the centre, and the four lashes meet under the frost mark like a frown line. Checked against flora.js itself, rendered in this page: identical. On top of that the eye is 10.6 units tall against Poppy's 14.8. At 46px it reads as two worried dark patches — the squint the 2026-09-27 rule forbids, from the other side. Any of H1–H3 fixes the direction; they differ in how much eye they give her.

5 · Expressions — idle, talking, pleased, asleep

Each hero is the flower button in its room, so each needs the four faces the button wears. Asleep is drawn at night with the creatures' own shut-eye arcs and Zs, because Winter's tuck-in has a sleeping face and the story's chapter scenes will ask for one.

IdleTalking PleasedAsleep

Pleased is the arc eye, asleep is the cup eye — upturned arcs over a big smile against the creatures' downturned arcs over a small one. They must never be mistaken for each other, and at 46px the mouth is what tells them apart.

6 · In the room

FALLOn Fall's real board — the trug, the damp earth, the orchard sky

Fall's board is brown on olive, so the hero has to be the brightest thing on it. Poppy has been standing here on a borrowed face since Turn 1, and her pink is the only non-autumn colour in the room. All three Fall candidates are drawn from Fall's own declared ramps — the orchard canopy, the gate's sky, the trug — so the flower finally belongs to the room it stands in. That cuts both ways for the Mop: its rust is the trug's own family, and on the woven centre cell it is the weakest figure of the three. The Dinnerplate's cream is the strongest.

SPRINGOn a plain spring-green stand-in

Spring has no board yet (it opens about Turn 6 and is designed as the nursery, doc 32). The planter here is Summer's, only so the hero has ground to stand on.

7 · Idle — the talking flower's sway and blink, hand-copied

The desk's pick for each season, breathing on the game's own idle: stem sway 3.4s, leaves 2.6s and 2.9s, blink every 5.4s, cheeks pulsing. Left of each pair: animated. Right: the same frame under reduced motion, which is what a player with the setting on sees — a still flower, every state still readable.

Two idle facts a build must carry. (1) Poppy's petals turn once every 26 seconds. That is invisible on eight identical round petals and on the sunflower's eighteen rays, but it turns a six-point daffodil into a pinwheel and spins a cup, a bell, a mop and a plate sideways — the spin is for radial heads only. (2) In the game the head sits outside the swaying stem, so only the stem moves. A bluebell's head hangs from its stem, so it has to ride the sway or it comes loose — the Bell is built that way on this page.

8 · Palettes — no new hex

Every colour on every candidate is a value docs/05 already declares — a :root token, a declared component ramp, or Poppy's and Holly's own. The ringed swatch is the dominant. The pair check is against Poppy (dominant: her pink, gp-talker) and Holly (dominant: the plum).

Which Fall and Spring pairs can stand together

The rule bites in Spring. Spring's natural colours — clear blue, violet, lemon — are all spoken for in doc 05: blue and violet are the rarity colours (Rare, Epic) and the gem, lemon is the coin and Legendary, and doc 05 forbids borrowing any of them. So the daffodil wears Poppy's face-gold, the bluebell wears Holly's frost-blue and reads cold, and the tulip wears the family's mouth crimson. Fall has no such problem — it has three declared autumn ramps of its own.

For the Spring hero you pick, may the build declare one component colour of its own, beside the drawing, the way Holly's plum was declared? It is the difference between a bluebell that is blue and one that is Winter's frost.

9 · Names, as the picker would show them

Poppy's and Holly's register: a real plant or a plain English word, one or two syllables. Every name below was checked against the game's data and the story bible — none is already a seed, a creature, a villager or a food. The picker's hero row is headed Who talks to you; it shows no names today, so the name under each disc is the one that would appear in the drawer and the chapter scenes. Ringed: the desk's pick.
