The Trunk and the Tree

One linear path, up the trunk. The badge reads a LEVEL; reputation fills toward it, and the rail itself never prints a number — a fill and an arrow, nothing else. Prisms buy everything on the path; gold never appears on a card. Nine frames, each a real 390×844 phone screen (frames 5 and 9 crop to smaller strips). Nothing here is wired to the game — every number, price and level shown is illustrative, picked to get pixels on screen, and the Prism economy itself is explicitly unsized. Nothing here decides anything the owner should decide.

This is a rewrite, not a second file — v2 stays in git history. The owner judged v2 by eye against a Monopoly Go reference and two Gossip Harbor screenshots (both kept off this machine, described here only in words) and ruled the surface a third time. Read the notes under each frame. Green = already ruled, this spike just follows it. Orange = an open question, with my recommendation marked as such. Blue = a placeholder I had to pick to draw anything at all — not a recommendation. Section 11 collects every question in one place, and section 12 says what was copied from which spike.

1 The first hour — levels 1–14

Full screen from the first tap into the tree. Marker just above the trickle and Daisy — both owned. Everything through level 14 is drawn real, per the brief; the curtain appears only right at the top edge, hinting the climb keeps going well past what a first session needs to see.

2 Mid-climb, around level 60

Same full-screen chrome, now mid-climb. One epic card up next (Word of Mouth — not legendary, see the note), a genuine legendary two cards up (the fifth lawn spot), dimmed under its own lock, and the curtain right above that. Below the marker: a maxed perk that has gone quiet, and an owned flower and creature with nothing left to buy.

3 The rarity ladder, side by side

Four different cards — a flower, a creature, a perk, a slot — all unlocked, all the same box. The only thing that changes is the ring and the colour. This is also where Word of Mouth's demotion and the fifth lawn spot's promotion sit next to each other, on purpose.

4 Inline detail, on the card itself

Daisy's own harvest perk, two stars in, exactly where it always lives on the path — no tap, no second screen. Shown next to a locked card above it and a bought, non-levelling access card below it, so the height difference between "compact" and "detailed" is visible in the same frame, not asserted in a caption.

5 The unveiling — a six-frame storyboard

Bluebell's card, at the moment the marker reaches level 9. v2 drew the four beats but not the feel — the owner's own words: an unlock that "feels like it's being unlocked," a pop, a reveal, something opening. Six frames this time: approach, contact, the mask cracking, the burst, an overshoot pop, and the stars landing.

6 The lawn — a pointer, not a picture

The one correction this pass carries. `tools/lawn-spike.html` already proved there is no spare surface on the yard band — it holds up to six creatures and two permanent floating buttons today, at the band's real height. So this is not a lawn board with generous room for perk slots; it is one small, honest drawing of the band as it already exists, plus a pointer.

7 The drone, in the Shop

The one card in this whole feature that is not on the path. Two prices: buy once with real money, or fly it for thirty minutes on a rewarded ad.

8 The garden scene — up next, and where auto-tap will sit

Doc 28's lesson — demand rendered on top of supply. The trunk's next card as a small badge the player sees while playing, without opening the tree at all. No "Auto-tap" pill — that never existed in the source — only a ghost button beside the flower marking where the real one will go, once it exists.

9 The order strip — Gossip Harbor's shape

The frame the owner most wants to see, and the one this pass gives the most care. One space at the top of the screen, three moments in a new player's first minutes: the tutorial quest bar teaches the loop, the first Turn arrives, and the same space then fills with customers and their orders — now drawn as a real horizontally-scrolling row of portraits, goods and rewards, not a chip. The flower teaches you to grow and to reopen the store; the store reopening is what brings the customers. Added 2026-09-27: the customer card's own reward area now carries two slots, a reputation wage every customer pays and an occasional tip most leave empty — frame 9C draws the strip, and a new frame 9D draws the tip both timing ways (seen up front, or hidden until delivery) for the owner to choose between.
