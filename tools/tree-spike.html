The Trunk and the Tree

One linear path, up the trunk. Reputation reveals; Prisms rank; gold buys. Eight frames, each a real 390×844 phone screen (frame 5 crops to a smaller filmstrip). Nothing here is wired to the game — every number, price and rung shown is illustrative, picked to get pixels on screen. Nothing here decides anything the owner should decide.

Read the notes under each frame. Green = already ruled, this spike just follows it. Orange = an open question, with my recommendation marked as such. Blue = a placeholder I had to pick to draw anything at all — not a recommendation. Section 10 collects every question in one place, and section 11 says what was copied from which spike.

1 The first hour — rungs 1–14

Drawn as a sheet — the Almanac's own chrome, since the tree replaces its petal tab. Reputation 3. Chapter I fires at the foot of the rail, rep 0; the marker sits just above it, at rung 3, with three unlocked cards below and the whole rest of the first hour laid out above, unmasked — the curtain is not drawn here on purpose (see the note).

2 Mid-climb, around rung 60

Drawn as a full screen instead — the alternative to section 1's sheet, so both are on the page to compare directly. One epic card up next, a legendary card visible above it dimmed (it still shows its five pips, empty), and the curtain two cards higher than that.

3 The rarity ladder, side by side

Four different cards — a flower, a creature, a flower, a perk — all unlocked, all the same box, same size. The only thing that changes is the ring and the colour. Judge whether that alone is enough to read "this one matters more."

4 One levelling card, open

A tap on "Daisy pays more" (Rich Bloom's first sub-node). Two of five stars bought. What it does now, what the next star adds, and the next star's Prisms price — all in the glossary's plain words, no percentages exposed on the card face itself.

5 The unveiling — a four-frame strip

Bluebell's card, at the moment the marker reaches rung 9. About two seconds in the real thing: masked → mask lifting → lit, stars appearing → settled. Cropped to the card and rail, not full phone chrome, so all four sit side by side.

6 The lawn — two slots, five slots

Doc 4's own diagram, drawn as a garden bed rather than a menu list. Perks are placed objects on the grass beside the blessed flower — owning and wearing are different things, the record shelf's equip grammar.

7 The drone, in the Shop

The one card in this whole feature that is not on the path. Two prices: buy once with real money, or fly it for thirty minutes on a rewarded ad.

8 Up next, on the garden itself

Doc 28's lesson — demand rendered on top of supply. The trunk's next card as a small badge the player sees while playing, without opening the tree at all.
