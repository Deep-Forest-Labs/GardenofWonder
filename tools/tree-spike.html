The Trunk and the Tree

One linear path, up the trunk. The badge reads a LEVEL; reputation fills toward it. Prisms rank; gold buys. Nine frames, each a real 390×844 phone screen (frame 5 crops to a smaller filmstrip). Nothing here is wired to the game — every number, price and level shown is illustrative, picked to get pixels on screen. Nothing here decides anything the owner should decide.

This is a rewrite, not a second file. The owner judged v1 by eye and ruled the surface — every fix below traces to a line in that review. Read the notes under each frame. Green = already ruled, this spike just follows it. Orange = an open question, with my recommendation marked as such. Blue = a placeholder I had to pick to draw anything at all — not a recommendation. Section 11 collects every question in one place, and section 12 says what was copied from which spike.

1 The first hour — levels 1–14

Full screen from the first tap into the tree. Marker at level 3 — Daisy and the overnight trickle already owned, Power Punch access up next. Everything through level 14 is drawn real, per the brief; the curtain appears only right at the top edge, hinting the climb keeps going well past what a first session needs to see.

2 Mid-climb, around level 60

Same full-screen chrome, now mid-climb. One epic card up next (Word of Mouth — not legendary, see the note), a genuine legendary two cards up (the fifth lawn spot), dimmed under its own lock, and the curtain right above that. Below the marker: an owned card that has hit its cap and gone quiet, and one still mid-level.

3 The rarity ladder, side by side

Four different cards — a flower, a creature, a perk, a slot — all unlocked, all the same box. The only thing that changes is the ring and the colour. This is also where Word of Mouth's demotion and the fifth lawn spot's promotion sit next to each other, on purpose.

4 Inline detail, on the card itself

"Daisy pays more," two stars in, exactly where it always lives on the path — no tap, no second screen. Shown next to a locked card above it and an owned, capped, non-levelling card below it, so the height difference between "compact" and "detailed" is visible in the same frame, not asserted in a caption.

5 The unveiling — a four-frame strip

Bluebell's card, at the moment the marker reaches level 9. About two seconds in the real thing: masked → mask lifting → lit, stars appearing → settled. Cropped to the card and rail, not full phone chrome, so all four sit side by side.

6 The lawn — three beats, then two/five slots

The lawn is not a screen and has no door. Spots sit right on the garden scene; tapping an empty one opens a picker — the same grammar as tapping an empty plot opens `seeds` today. Three beats, then the two-slot and five-slot comparison, drawn on the grass itself rather than inside a menu.

7 The drone, in the Shop

The one card in this whole feature that is not on the path. Two prices: buy once with real money, or fly it for thirty minutes on a rewarded ad.

8 The garden scene — up next, and where auto-tap will sit

Doc 28's lesson — demand rendered on top of supply. The trunk's next card as a small badge the player sees while playing, without opening the tree at all. No "Auto-tap" pill — that never existed in the source — only a ghost button beside the flower marking where the real one will go, once it exists.

9 The quest strip converting

The frame the owner most wants to see. One space at the top of the screen, three moments in a new player's first minutes: the tutorial quest bar teaches the loop, the first Turn arrives, and the same box then fills with customers and their orders. The flower teaches you to grow and to reopen the store; the store reopening is what brings the customers.
