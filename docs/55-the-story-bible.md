# The Story Bible — volume one

**Status: DIRECTION, 2026-09-26 — the owner is picking. Not a spec, and no game code follows from
it until the owner has read §7 aloud and ruled §9.** This is the story that rides the reputation
trunk: the chapters, the people, the rules of the voice, what each chapter visibly changes, and
the first session's actual lines. It is built from the owner's premise
([53-narrative-premise.md](53-narrative-premise.md) — the **what**) and the flow brief
([54-the-story-flow.md](54-the-story-flow.md) — the **when and where**, which the owner ruled is
*"just a reference for how we're building the game and can be adjusted"*). Where the two pulled
apart, this document kept the premise and flagged the pull in §9.

Pressure-tested before filing by two independent critics — a market critic (§1, §2, §7 against the
review evidence) and a game-fit critic (§4, §6, §7 against docs 32, 47, 52 and 54). What they found
and what changed is at the end.

**Every claim in §1 carries a link or is marked JUDGMENT. Every level number in §4 is a placeholder
for the spec**: the reputation curve is being rebalanced to the flowers' pace (2026-09-26), so no
chapter can be pinned to an exact level yet.

**The words.** Everything a player reads uses the glossary at the top of
[32-the-garden-year.md](32-the-garden-year.md) as it stands today: **Prisms** (never "Saved Seeds",
never "seeds"), **a catch** (Dewkissed, Gilded, **Radiant**, Wonderstruck — never "mutation" or
"Prismatic"), **the Turn** (never "reset"), and **levels** — a chapter sits at a *reputation level*,
and reputation is what fills the gap between levels. The customers' counter is **the Garden Stand**,
its on-screen name.

## 1. What this lane's players praise and punish in a story

**Method and limits.** Web research, 2026-09-26: store reviews (Apple's review pages and
justuseapp.com, which mirrors them), industry teardowns, ad-creative analyses, regulator rulings and
press. **Reddit could not be fetched** — the tool is blocked there — so no community thread is cited,
and several review aggregators refused the connection. Claims seen only in a search snippet are
marked **[snippet]**. Short review fragments are quoted as evidence; everything else is paraphrased.
The five games: Lily's Garden, Gossip Harbor, Merge Mansion, June's Journey, and **Love & Pies**,
chosen as the fifth because it has a mystery plot, a single-mother lead with a young daughter, and
the most story-specific reviews of the candidates (Homescapes had more reviews, almost all about its
ads).

story beats are short (**JUDGMENT** — an argument from absence).

### Child-coded characters, and childlessness

- **Baby loss as a fail state was banned on one complaint.** The ASA upheld a complaint against
  *Happy Hospital* (DragonPlus, February 2025): ghost-baby icons and "FAILED" over pregnant women made
  miscarriage or stillbirth "appear trivial" and caused unjustified distress
  ([ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/dragonplus-game-ltd-a24-1268671-dragonplus-game-ltd.html)).
  Ads showing children coming to harm are also banned ([ASA guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/fear-and-distress.html)).
- **Infertility on screen is welcomed when it is honest and punished when a "miracle baby" fixes it**
  — the trope that implies waiting and trying will make it happen
  ([Pregnantish](https://www.pregnantish.com/articles/infertility-on-tv-who-gets-it-right-and-who-gets-it-wrong);
  [WIN](https://winfertility.com/blog/infertility-on-screen-does-televisions-portrayal-of-infertility-help-or-hurt/)).
- **The substitute child is on the same list as the miracle baby.** "Get a dog" is a stock entry on
  infertility's *things never to say* lists, and childless writers push back on pets offered as
  substitutes ([HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/21-things-you-should-never-say-to-someone-struggling-with-infertility_b_9768032);
  [Love in the Little Things](https://loveinthelittlethingsblog.com/2015/09/14/no-im-sorry-but-a-dog-is-not-an-alternative-to-a-child/);
  [Childless by Marriage](https://childlessbymarriageblog.com/2019/02/28/no-my-dog-is-not-my-child-substitute/)).
  A flower that calls a childless woman its mother **is** that consolation if the story ever says so —
  which collides with the owner's own phrase, *"the daughter she never had"*. §5 and §9 (question 16)
  carry it.
- **"Mommy" is also slang.** In 2026 internet usage the word carries an innuendo
  ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/dommy-mommy)); a child's voice saying it beside a
  physical imperative ("tap me", "harder") is a mockery clip, not an angry review — and mockery sticks.
- **Choice is what the community praised** — brands offering Mother's Day opt-outs were thanked by
  the infertility and loss communities ([Scary Mommy](https://www.scarymommy.com/lifestyle/mothers-day-opt-out-emails)).
- **Inside a game, restraint went unremarked.** Lily's Garden carries an infant loss in a single
  letter **[snippet]** ([wiki](https://lilysgarden.fandom.com/wiki/Day_29)); no review reacting to it
  could be found, either way.
- **No casual game with infertility at its centre was found**, with review evidence either way. We
  would be first. That is the opportunity and the exposure in one sentence.
- **The line has two cultural anchors.** The cosy one: P.D. Eastman's *Are You My Mother?* (1960), a
  hatchling asking everyone it meets, on the NEA's top-100 children's books
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Are_You_My_Mother%3F)). The horror one: Doctor Who's
  *"Are you my mummy?"* (*The Empty Child*, 2005), a creepy-child episode
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empty_Child)) — and British English would localise the
  premise's line into exactly that string.

### Five rules this bible obeys because the market said so

1. **A beat every session; never a drought, and never let the writing fall behind.** Every winner
   puts story in the first sessions; the anger is at gaps — Merge Mansion's 27 days, June's Journey's
   four to five, Lily's Garden's players outrunning the writers. → Chapters stay front-loaded, but
   from Chapter V on **a story line rides every level** (§4), and volume two is written before heavy
   players can reach Chapter VIII (§8).
2. **The heroine acts; she never merely suffers, and she never looks like a careless parent.**
   Players punish a whiny lead and one who neglects her child; regulators and researchers punish
   helpless women in ads. → Mara is competent and dry, she wins, she refuses. **The flower is never
   harmed by her absence** — it cannot wilt, starve, fail or die, and no ad or scene asks anyone to
   "save" it (§5).
3. **Continuity is sacred; never reward the betrayer.** The Gossip Harbor one-star cluster is retcons
   and a cheating ex rewarded. → This bible is the canon; Mara's ex never returns as a romance; Julian
   is never rewarded for what he does in volume one.
4. **Dark themes, with dignity — never gamified, never trivial, never the only register.** Lily's
   Garden is loved for adult subjects; the ASA banned the gamified version; Love & Pies players tire of
   trauma on repeat; infertility writers condemn the miracle baby. → The restraint rules (§5); comedy
   carried by Delphine and Bram; and **the flower never fixes Mara's wound and is never offered as a
   substitute** — no line, ever, says she has a child now.
5. **The ad must be the game, and the mystery must pay.** "Does not match the ads" is a review title
   and now a regulator's ruling; Merge Mansion's unpaid mystery is its loudest complaint. → **The first
   minute of play is the ad**: the question and the deflection, exactly as in §7. And the mystery pays
   in slivers from the first session (§4).

### The honest risk: is *"Are you my mommy?"* the line that cuts through, or the screenshot?

**No review anywhere reacts to this exact line in a cosy game — so the answer is about conditions,
and the evidence on conditions is clear.**

**It cuts through** when the question belongs to the creature, not to her grief — a newborn's naive
curiosity, Eastman's hatchling — when her childlessness stays in the backstory, told with restraint,
when she answers on her own terms, and when the creature does not "fix" her.

**It gets screenshotted with anger** when it is framed around a sad, childless woman waiting to be
rescued (the "Help the Girl" pattern, now under the ASA, the NAD and academic study); when her
infertility is a twist or a shock; when the child-coded creature can suffer or fail through neglect —
the combination the ASA upheld against on a single complaint (**JUDGMENT** that it transfers, tied to
that ruling); when anything frames the flower as her substitute child; when it sits beside a physical
imperative a slang reading can seize on; and, for a UK audience, when it is **repeated** — once, soft, from something plainly a
flower, it reads as Eastman; as a pleading refrain it reads as *The Empty Child* (**JUDGMENT**).

**The verdict: keep the line. It is the hook — on the condition that the rules in §5 hold without
exception.** The flower says it once. Mara answers with her name, by the player's own tap, in the tone she
chooses. The flower never suffers for her absence and is never called her child. No physical imperative
sits near it. Nothing near it sells. The ad shows exactly that first minute and never a crying Mara. And
the exact word — *mommy*, *mama* — is decided for every market before launch (§9, question 15). Break any one of those and the same line becomes the screenshot.

## 2. The premise, fitted to the game

**The sentence:** *Mara, who wanted a child and didn't get one, inherits her grandmother's garden
and a seed tin — and the flower that grows from it thinks she is its mother, and remembers things
it cannot possibly know.*

### The feeling underneath — ruled 2026-09-26

**The heart of the story is raising something that grows up and needs you a little less each Year.**
Poppy ages from toddler-curious to wry across the chapters (§5), and the player watches it happen. That
feeling reaches three kinds of player at once: the woman who wanted children and didn't have them
(Mara's story), the mother whose children have grown and gone (**JUDGMENT** — likely a large share of a
35–55 audience, not measured), and anyone who was the aunt, the godmother or the teacher who helped raise
someone.

**Mara's childlessness is the private wound underneath it, never the headline.** It is implied — the
practised answer to Delphine, the lullaby she stops humming, the half-painted nursery nobody mentions —
and said aloud once, in four lines, in volume two. *"She wanted a child and it didn't happen"* is left
open on purpose: a woman who miscarried, one who could not conceive and one whose life never allowed it
can each read her own story in it without the game naming any of them. **The player may feel Poppy is
the daughter Mara never had; no character, line or ad ever says so** (the owner's answer to §9 question
16, after §1's substitute-child evidence).

**Considered and rejected the same day: Mara's own daughter died.** Not too dark for the lane — Lily's
Garden carries an infant loss — but wrong for this story. Asked of a bereaved mother, *"Are you my
mama?"* turns from tender to cruel; players would read Poppy as the dead girl's soul returned, which
tips the story toward a ghost story and swallows Gran's mystery; it gives the story two lost daughters
competing with each other; it makes Poppy a replacement child, the substitute problem at its sharpest;
and the backstory could never be shown in an ad (§1's rule 5). The darker weight is already carried
where the premise put it: Gran is gone, and Gran gave away a daughter she spent thirty years trying to
hold onto. A bereaved Mara would need a new opening and a new hook, and would be chosen deliberately if
ever.

Kept whole from the owner's premise: Mara; the flower's question and her deflection; the
inheritance with its one-year clause; the show circuit; Gran Ophelia present only through memory;
the five-beat childlessness thread and every restraint rule; the flower that never sells; the
sympathetic cousin; the given-away daughter as the mystery, alive, for volume two.

Fitted, per doc 53's read and doc 54's flow:

- **The will's one-year clause is the first Year.** Gran's will leaves Mara the garden *if it lives
  through one Year*. The first Turn — minutes into the first session — is the clause met: the
  garden is hers, and she reopens Gran's Garden Stand. The Turn gets a story meaning it never had.
- **The clause being met early changes Julian, and improves him.** In the premise the clause is a
  year of pressure. Here it ends in session one, so the cousin stops being a rival heir and becomes
  a man who has already lost the garden and still needs money — who then learns what grew from the
  tin. That is the premise's own "desperate, not villainous", arrived at sooner.
- **Factions become people at the Stand.** One reputation number (doc 52). Delphine, Bram and the
  Baroness are villagers who place orders and whose chapters arrive at levels — never three tracks.
- **Gran's note carries the stake past session one.** The clause is met, but her note says *don't sell
  it — not even to family* (§7): the player leaves the first session with a question, and Julian's
  arrival in Chapter V answers it.
- **The pressure is interpersonal, not criminal.** No underworld, no cartel, no bought judge: the
  circuit is genteel and snobbish, Julian owes a rich collector rather than a gang, and the
  Baroness wants the flower the way collectors want things. The lane does soap, not crime (doc 53
  collision 6), and none of it needs a content descriptor the game does not already carry.
- **The show is where the garden's catches become dangerous.** The flower is rooted and cannot be
  entered in a show; Mara's blooms can. The garden grows catches — Radiant, Wonderstruck — that the
  circuit has not seen in forty years, and the judges want to know where the seed came from. That
  is the premise's "Wonder wins everything, which is exactly the wrong kind of attention", moved
  onto a mechanic the game already has.
- **The game already carries the mystery.** The flower already hums — `Sound.sing()`, three phrases on
  the ambience channel, heard by default — and doc 48 already makes that hum the First Record's melody,
  *"the garden's own lullaby"*. It becomes Gran's lullaby, and nobody taught it. The album's **The Open Question** set already names *The Locked
  Greenhouse*, *What the Flower Won't Say*, *Someone Has Been Weeding* and *Not Yet*. The story
  gives existing things their reason; it invents little.

### The flower's name — Poppy (ruled 2026-09-26)

The owner ruled **Poppy** the same day this bible was written (decision log, the naming entry): the
flower of *remembrance*, for a plant whose whole mystery is memories that are not its own. The bible
writes Poppy everywhere the premise says Wonder, and keeps one idea from its own draft: **the flower
names itself** in session one — *"I'm Poppy."* / *"Who told you that?"* / *"Nobody. I just know it."* —
so the name is the first thing it remembers that nobody taught it. Who first called her Poppy is volume
two's to answer.

### The perfume thread — the owner's word, 2026-09-27

**The owner:** *"Not only are we creating magnificent flowers, but we're creating amazing perfumes and
colognes, and that could spin into the story, into the fashion world, which is also a women's
category… how there could be competitions for perfume… and how we're making the most unique perfumes in
the world. I think that should be a big part of the story because I do want to add a place where players
can mix different essences together to create new perfumes."* The game has carried this since August —
the apiary's honey and wax, the Apothecary's *Petal Perfume* (*"Pressed petals set in beeswax"*), doc
26's premium shelf of perfumes, candles and salves — and the story now gives it a reason.

**The idea that ties it in: scent is memory.** Smell cues memories that are more vivid, more emotional and
older than sight does — most from the first ten years of life
([Chu & Downes, *Proust nose best*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12184552/);
[review, *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-018-1545-3)).
Poppy is the remembrance flower whose whole mystery is memories that are not her own. **Perfume is memory
you can carry.** So:

- **Gran was a perfumer as well as a gardener.** The glasshouse was her still room; the album's *Locked
  Greenhouse* is where she worked. The village remembers how she smelled and nobody could ever name it.
- **The seed in the tin is the note the fragrance world has chased for forty years** — a scent nobody has
  been able to make. That is why a collector wants Poppy: not the flower, the scent that comes of her.
- **The circuit becomes two worlds.** The village show (flowers) grows a scent class, and the scent class
  leads to the city: **the Scent Prize**, and an old fragrance house whose head is the Baroness. Fashion
  is the soap register's natural home — glamorous, genteel and quietly vicious — and it keeps the pressure
  interpersonal.
- **Mara makes perfumes** from what the garden grows, the bees give and the season leaves on a bloom — and
  they become things the Stand sells, the shows judge and the fashion world notices.
- **The volume-two hook gets sharper.** In Chapter VIII a rival's perfume arrives from the city, and Poppy
  knows it: it is Gran's. Someone alive has Gran's nose. The given-away daughter is a perfumer.

**The place to mix perfumes is a feature, not a story beat.** It needs its own direction document and a
spike before any code (the wireframe gate, doc 34), and §9 questions 18–21 put its shape to the owner. This
bible says what the story needs from it and **never makes a chapter depend on it until it is built**: the
chapter's required change stays on the counter (§4), and the perfumery's opening is the headline once it
exists. What the story needs, for that spec: an essence from each flower; honey to sweeten and beeswax for
the base; a catch — Radiant, Wonderstruck — as a rare note; a few essences mixed into a perfume the player
names and keeps in a book; perfumes as Stand goods and show entries. **No character flower is ever an
ingredient** (§5).

**The lane has the theme and nobody owns it with a story.** A cosy perfume-workshop game on Steam mixes
essences to customers' moods ([Perfume Atelier](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3962270/Perfume_Atelier/));
a mobile merge-and-makeover game counts perfumes among its beauty items
([Glow Tales](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/6754087834)). **JUDGMENT:** none pairs perfume-making with a
story, and none ties scent to memory — worth a proper research pass before the perfumery's spec, as §1 was
for the story.

## 4. The chapters — volume one

Eight chapters, the premise's Acts 1–3. Volume one ends where Act 3 ends: the memories have begun,
the Baroness has made her offer, and the flower has said the one thing that makes the second volume
necessary. The confession (beat 5) and the daughter reveal are volume two.

**Spacing: front-loaded, as the brief proposed — with one condition §1's evidence adds.** Every
winner in the lane puts story in the first sessions, so five chapters sit in the first fifth of the
climb. But the lane's loudest story anger is the **drought** — Merge Mansion's 27-day wait, June's
Journey's four to five days, Lily's Garden's players outrunning the writers — and three chapters across
the last four fifths would be exactly that. So the back three are spread evenly, and **a line of story
is bound to almost every level** (*Between chapters*, below). **The exact levels are the spec's to
place**: the curve is being rebalanced.

| Chapter | Placeholder level | Act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I — The Seed Tin | 1, closing on the **first Turn** | 1 |
| II — Someone Has Been Weeding | ~4 | 1 |
| III — Over the Fence | ~8 | 1 |
| IV — The Village Show | ~14 | 2 |
| V — The Cousin | ~22 | 2 |
| VI — The Baroness Pays a Call | ~50 | 2 → 3 |
| VII — What the Flower Won't Say | ~80 | 3 |
| VIII — Not Yet | ~110 | 3 |

**Order is a rule, not a hope.** The shipped quest steps pay reputation, so level 2 — even level 4 —
can arrive mid-tutorial. **Nothing but Chapter I plays until Chapter I has been seen, and chapters play
strictly in order**, whatever level the player has reached.

**The visible spine: Gran's Garden Stand, drawn on the order strip.** The one thing every player sees
every session is the order strip the quest strip becomes at the first Turn (ruled 2026-09-26). The
story draws **the Stand's counter** along it, and the counter restores across the volume: shutters
up (I), the sign repainted (III), a show ribbon (IV), window boxes full (VI), a child's night-light,
lit (VII), and Gran's music box (VIII). It is the renovation reveal the lane loves, on the one surface a
phone always shows — and it fits the strip's ruled shape (2026-09-26, doc 52's third REVISED block):
customers sit behind their items *on a counter*. Because that strip scrolls sideways, **the restored
pieces sit on the counter's fixed end, the Stand's own stall front**, never on a stretch that scrolls
off. **New art: one strip-height SVG with six states** (§8). *(The draft put this on a
house on Summer's far hill; the game-fit critic showed the garden board covers the far hills on a
phone, so a lit window there would be a few pixels nobody sees. The house survives as §9's question 9,
pending a 390px screenshot.)*

**What a chapter is, in length** — measured from the owner's recording of a top-grossing merge-story
game (the recording stays on the owner's machine; described here in words): a scene is **10–12 lines,
no line over about 15 words, 110–130 words in all**, two faces trading bubbles, ending on the lead's
private thought. A chapter here is **one or two such scenes**.

### I — The Seed Tin

**Level 1 → the first Turn. The opening act; §7 is its full text.** Mara arrives at a garden that is
hers only if it lives through one Year; she plants the one seed in Gran's tin, and it blooms, looks up
at her, and asks if she is its mother. She says her name. The flower teaches her the garden and asks
who used to ring the bell at the Stand; she decides to open it. At the first Turn the Year is kept,
Gran's note says ring the bell and don't sell it — *not even to family* — and Delphine is the first face
at the counter.

- **Visible change:** the quest strip becomes the order strip, the Stand's shutters go up, and
  Delphine's face is the only one on it (the first Turn fills **one** slot — §6). Fall's gate lifts at
  the same Turn, as it already does.
- **Thread:** beat 1 — the question, and *"…I'm Mara."* Nothing explained.
- **Slivers planted:** the flower hums a tune someone sang to it (Gran's lullaby); it names itself;
  Gran's note warns her off family — the question the player carries into day two.
- **Words:** ~200 (§7).
- **The act break** closes on a latch — the first Turn completed and the scene not yet seen — after the

### II — Someone Has Been Weeding

### Between chapters — slivers and story lines

One line of flower speech at a quiet moment, bound to a level, never with art. Two kinds, because they
need different rules:

- **Memory slivers — about twenty.** Gran's life in fragments the player can't place yet: *"Someone used
  to cut the roses on Sundays."* · *"The bees know me. That's odd, isn't it."* · *"I dreamt about a blue
  door."* They are part of the mystery, so **they queue in order and are never skipped**; one a day at
  most.
- **Story lines — about ninety, one bound to almost every level that has no chapter.** The flower's
  running commentary on what the chapters set moving — Julian's visits, the Baroness's letters, the
  night-light. They are texture, so **when several are owed, only the newest plays** and the rest are
  superseded; two a day at most. A fast player skips chatter, never a memory or a chapter.
- **The first line after Chapter I** plants Holly: *"There's another one in the tin. It says 'wait for
  frost'."*

## 5. The rules of the voice

**The flower speaks to the player as Mara.** It calls her *mama* (ruled 2026-09-26, §9 question 15) exactly once — the hook, in session
one — and after she says her name it calls her Mara from then on. It learned. That single use is the
restraint that keeps the line from becoming a nag, and it is the only place the word lives until
volume two.

**How it ages — on chapters, not on Turns.** The premise ages the flower across bloom cycles. Here
the voice is staged by **chapter**, because Turns carry no required story (doc 54 §3) and a player
who Turns often would otherwise hear a teenager in Chapter II. Flagged as a pull in §9.

| Stage | Chapters | The voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Toddler-curious | I–II | Short questions, repetition, delight. *"Mara. Mara-Mara."* No baby-talk spellings, ever |
| Child | III–V | Opinions, names for things, jokes that don't quite land, first unease |
| Wry | VI–VIII | Observant and dry; sometimes says the adult thing Mara won't. Never cruel |

Only the new story lines are staged. The shared mood buckets stay one set.

**The restraint rules, from the premise, verbatim:**

> Mara wanted a child and it did not happen. That is the wound the FTUE lands on, and it is why the
> flower's question works instead of merely being cute. It is never stated up front and never used as
> a monetization lever.

> Restraint rules: beat 5 is four lines of dialogue maximum. Mara never explains herself, never
> monologues about it, and no other character offers consolation on the subject. The restraint is
> what makes it land, and it is also what keeps it from reading as exploitative.

> Mara never explains her childlessness. Imply it. One spoken confession in the whole script, four
> lines, then silence.

**Added by this bible:**

- **The flower never sells.** Already the house rule (doc 37) and doc 53's hard rule: the flower —
  its face, its voice, its name — never appears in a purchase prompt, an ad placement, an offer or a
  store surface. The moments dialog sells nothing (doc 47).
- **No child is used to point at the wound.** Tobin (*"It's for my mum. Don't tell her."*) stays
  exactly as he is. No scene puts him next to Mara's silence.
- **Nothing that needs a descriptor.** No alcohol (Delphine brings tea), no crime, no romance past a
  look (Theo), no loss described. Grief is Gran, already gone.
- **Other characters hear a plant.** The flower speaks only to Mara (the premise's rule), so every
  villager scene is a place for dry comedy: *"Who are you talking to, love?"*
- **The flower is never harmed by Mara's absence.** It cannot wilt, starve, sleep sadly or fail, and
  no scene, notification or ad asks anyone to save it. A child-coded character suffering for a
  parent's absence, beside an infertility story, is the combination the ASA banned on one complaint
  (§1); §9 puts it to the owner as a ruling, because the game's stakes are his.
- **The flower never fixes the wound, and Mara never takes the title.** No line, in this volume or the
  next, says Mara has a child now, calls the flower her daughter, or offers it as a consolation. The miracle-baby resolution is the one infertility writers condemn (§1), and the restraint
  that makes the question land is the same restraint that keeps it from being answered.
- **No physical imperatives near the word.** The flower never tells her to tap it, touch it, go harder
  or do it again — in the opening run above all, where the question is still on screen. The slang reading
  (§1) needs only one clip.
- **Villagers hear a hum, never words.** The flower speaks only to Mara; everyone else hears a plant
  that hums. That is what keeps *"Who are you talking to, love?"* comedy, and not a woman imagining a
  child.
- **Mara's thoughts are dry, too.** The scene's thought bubble shows what she holds back — *Said that
  well.* — and never names the wound. It may show a thing was practiced; it never explains why.
- **No character flower is ever an ingredient.** Poppy, Holly and the Fall and Spring heroes are never
  distilled, pressed, mixed or sold as a scent, in play or in story. The fragrance house wants to *own* the
  source, never to extract it. A girl whose essence is bottled is the premise of Süskind's *Perfume*, a
  murder novel; nothing in this story may rhyme with it.
- **Fashion is never cruel about bodies.** The fashion world can be snobbish about taste, money and
  pedigree; never about weight, age or looks, as a joke or as a stake (**JUDGMENT**, for this audience).
- **A competition is a chapter, not an event that never ends.** The Scent Prize and the shows are story
  beats and, at most, seasonal showcases — never the endless, unwinnable timed ladder that is the lane's
  third-largest complaint (doc 38, 17%).
- **The ex never returns as a romance.** §1's rule 3. He stays offstage and in the past.
- **The ad is the first minute.** The UA creative is the owner's, but the bible's line is this: it
  shows the question and the deflection exactly as §7 plays them — never a crying Mara, never the
  flower in danger, never a scene the game does not contain.
- **The glossary rule** above, in every line.

**The existing lines, re-read rather than rewritten.** `FLOWER_LINES`' mood buckets already sound
like a delighted small child — *"Sparkles everywhere!"*, *"That one shook the roots!"*, *"Look up — the
sky has colours!"* — so they stay as the flower's shared voice across all three stages. About fifteen
change: the lines that address the player as a stranger or a customer — `greet` (*"Well hello,
gardener!"* reads as a shop host, not a child who knows her), a few `idle` lines, *"The basket runneth
over!"* — and three tap lines the slang reading could seize on (*"Ooh, do that again!"*, *"Keep it
coming!"*, *"Tickles!"*). That is a one-hour writing pass, not a rewrite. The weather, catch and Turn lines are untouched.

## 6. How a beat is delivered — on surfaces that exist

The surfaces are doc 54 §4's table. A beat that needs anything else is marked **ENGINEERING**.

| Surface | What the story puts there |
| --- | --- |
| **Chapter ticks on the rail** | Each chapter is a tick at its level with its title, **never a card**, and its level holds no card (doc 52). Tapping a played tick replays the scene without re-celebrating — a new rail interaction the tree spec owns |
| **The moments dialog** (doc 47) | The chapter scene, as a new moment kind (below) |
| **The flower's lines** | The opening run (the `hollyIntro` pattern — walked in order, consumed only after the last line draws), memory slivers, story lines, the re-voiced greetings |
| **Villagers' order lines** | Each arriving villager's greet, waiting and delivered lines, and a few chapter-keyed lines on existing villagers |
| **The welcome-back board** | After three days away, one line from the villager who arrived most recently. Nothing more: reputation is never earned while away, so no chapter can be crossed there, and doc 47 already rules that a held beat surfaces without a welcome line |
| **The quest strip** | Chapter I only. It becomes the order strip at the first Turn and is never story again |

### The lane's scene shape — from the owner's recording, in words

The owner recorded a minute of a top-grossing merge-story game to show how long a beat is; the recording
stays on his machine. What it shows:

- **Two faces, stacked bubbles.** Round portraits with a name tag, one character's bubbles on the left,
  the lead's on the right in her own colour; each new line pushes the stack up, three or four visible at
  once, over the room behind, blurred.
- **Short lines.** About six to fifteen words each; interruptions with a dash; mood carried by swapping
  the portrait's expression, not by stage directions.
- **The lead's private thought.** The scene ends on a cloud-shaped bubble — what she thinks and doesn't
  say — and a Continue button.
- **A skip button** in the corner, always.
- **A title card between scenes** — a full-screen "new day" card with the lead's full-length art — is the
  whole cutscene. Between the two scenes, a small visible change (a sign placed on the street).

**Two scenes and a change in about sixty seconds.** That is the unit this bible's chapters are sized to,
and the thought bubble is the most useful thing in it for this story: **it lets the player see what Mara
holds back without her ever saying it** — restraint made visible.

### What needs building, priced

- **ENGINEERING, medium — the chapter scene, in the lane's shape. Recommended for volume one.** A
  `chapter` moment kind with its own entry in `pendingMoments()`: two portraits, stacked bubbles, Mara's
  thought bubble, lines that page on tap, skip always, Continue at the end. Today `showMoment` builds
  only seed and upgrade cards — a title, bullets and a button — so a scene does not fit in it as it
  stands. Needs Mara's portrait (§3).
- **BUILT 2026-09-27 (gate 2, the engine) — ENGINEERING, small — chapters leave the moments session cap.** A chapter
  is `Game.storyPending()`, not a moment kind, so `sessionCap` never sees it; it counts against

  seed reveals before the Turn; three of them exhaust `sessionCap`, and no queue position helps once
  `momentReady()` says no. Chapters count only against the story's own daily cap.
- **ENGINEERING, small — the first Turn makes room for the act break.** Chapter I closes on a latch
  (Turns completed ≥ 1, scene not seen) once the ceremony sheet is shut. **Fall's "Swipe left" coach mark
  waits until the scene is dismissed** — while a coach mark is up, no moment can open. And **the first Turn

  surprise me."*); the other two arrive on the normal refill. Today `turnYear()` regenerates every slot.
- **ENGINEERING, small — a reply chip for Mara.** Under the flower's bubble, one or two buttons with
  Mara's line on them. Never a branch: two chips differ in tone and lead to the same next line. The

- **ENGINEERING, small — the opening run and the coach take turns.** The flower's bubble is suppressed

  lines stay silent while the run plays.
- **ENGINEERING, small — the flower's birth from the tin.** Today the talking flower is on screen from
  frame one; the opening needs its centre cell to start as soil and grow once.
- **BUILT 2026-09-27 (gate 2) — ENGINEERING, small — villagers by chapter.** `CUSTOMERS` rows carry `chapter`;
  their pools and every chapter-keyed line come from the script through `Game.customerLines()`; a
  scripted first order (`order:` in `@change`) is dealt the moment its chapter is seen. Today a customer is picked at random by `minTier` and a
  good at random by tier. A villager needs a chapter gate and a scripted first arrival carrying a good
  `standGoodsAt(tier)` allows; chapter-keyed lines on existing villagers need a new data shape, since
  `lines` are fixed arrays.
- **ENGINEERING, small — the lullaby's cut-off.** The flower's hum (`Sound.sing()`, three phrases on the
  ambience channel, heard by default — unlike the music, which ships off) is Gran's lullaby; doc 48
  already makes it the First Record's melody. Chapter V needs it to stop mid-phrase.
- **BUILT 2026-09-27 (gate 2) — ENGINEERING, small — the daily caps.** `state.story.day`, rolled on `todayKey()`. A saved counter keyed to `todayKey()`, per kind.

### The rules every beat obeys — the records' laws (doc 49), applied to story

- **Never missable.** A chapter is a condition-latched sweep, not an event: *Chapter I seen, every
  earlier chapter seen, reached level ≥ this chapter's level, not yet seen* → queue it, checked at load
  and after every reputation gain.
- **Celebrates once.** The seen-flag is consumed only after the scene has drawn (doc 47).
- **In order.** Nothing but Chapter I before Chapter I is seen; chapters strictly in sequence; memory
  slivers in sequence; story lines superseded by newer ones.
- **Daily caps, per kind.** One chapter, one memory sliver, two story lines per local day. A chapter or a
  sliver over the cap waits, latched, for the next day's first quiet moment.
- **The first Turn carries Chapter I's close. Every Turn after it carries no required story.** A line at
  a later ceremony is flavour, never a chapter.
- **Perfumes join the Stand only once they can be made.** The Stand's pools know flowers and honey
  today; perfumes join when the perfumery exists, and never before — the never-ask-the-unproducible rule.
- **Story pays nothing but the change.** No chapter or line moves gold, gems, Prisms or reputation —
  reputation is never paid by the meta (2026-09-24). A story record is **song-only**, so the story never
  becomes a power source.
- **Every scene can be skipped, and replayed from its tick.**

**The record shelf is not built** (doc 49, gate 1, held). Chapter VIII's record is a bonus on top of its
visible change, never the change itself.

### The sim-test invariants

1. **Never missable** — after load and after every reputation gain, the next chapter owed (by order and
   level) is seen or queued, including on a save that crossed its level before the story existed.
2. **Celebrates once** — the seen-flag is consumed only after the scene draws; no sweep re-queues it.
3. **In order** — nothing but Chapter I before Chapter I is seen; chapters and memory slivers in sequence.
4. **The daily caps** — at most one chapter, one sliver and two lines drawn per local day; a chapter or
   sliver over the cap persists and surfaces the next day; a superseded line is never drawn.
5. **Outside the session cap** — a chapter is never refused by `DATA.moments.sessionCap`.
6. **Never on a card** — no chapter's level equals any card's level on the path.
7. **None on a Turn after the first** — Chapter I's close is owed once, when Turns completed first
   reaches 1; no later Turn queues story.
8. **The story pays nothing** — no chapter or line moves gold, gems, Prisms or reputation.
9. **It survives the Turn** — `state.story` sits in bill 1's SURVIVES column.
10. **The flower never reaches a store surface** — no story line, flower line or flower art is referenced
    from shop, offer or ad code (a grep check, in the style of the charm whitelist).

  Again!"* and *"Do it harder now"* within a minute of *mommy*. All three are gone, a rule forbids
