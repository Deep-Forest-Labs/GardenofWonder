# Market Refresh — the lane as it stands, 2026-08-30

**Status: research, 2026-08-30. Extends and corrects [17-market-and-positioning.md](17-market-and-positioning.md);
it does not replace it.** Doc 17 is the foundation and most of it holds. Where this document disagrees, it
says so and shows the source.

Every number here carries a link or is marked **JUDGMENT**. Where a figure could not be sourced, it says
so rather than guessing. Twenty-three agents ran this: nine on competitors, three on benchmarks and rules,
five on channels, two on money, three adversarial fact-checkers, and one critic whose job was to find what
everyone missed. The fact-checkers corrected thirteen claims and killed four outright; those are marked.

## Read this first — what actually changed since doc 17

**Doc 17 is fifteen days old, not a year old.** Nothing in the *market* moved in a fortnight. So this is not
a "the world changed" document, and anyone who tells you it is has not checked the date. What this refresh
actually buys is three things:

1. **Corrections.** Six of doc 17's load-bearing numbers do not survive checking. Two of them are anchors
   the whole strategy leans on.
2. **Width.** Fourteen games that matter, that doc 17 does not name — including the one whose store page
   is our positioning almost word for word, and the one that just died doing it.
3. **The complaint pile.** 2,650 store reviews read and coded. That is the part that is worth money.

produces the original, and see "the graveyard is a mobile statistic" below.

### The one methodological change that matters

**Sensor Tower bought AppMagic on 2026-05-13**, having already bought data.ai in 2024

```
                            └──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                            │
  ---------------------------- the honesty line -------------------------------
                                            │

  no ads or opt-in only     │  Window Garden · Florisium · Usagi   │
  4.8 stars, no money       │  Shima · Bloom Idle · Tiny Harvest   │
                            │  Kinder World (DEAD, Oct 2025)       │
                            └──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                            │
  ABANDONED                 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐

  to come back              │  Viridi (delisted from iOS)          │
                            └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Three tiers, and **nobody is in the middle**. The funded games are hated for how they treat you and make
money anyway. The loved games are ad-free and earn nothing. The abandoned games got the reach and never
built a reason to return. **The middle square — loved *and* earning — is empty, and it is where the Year
points.** That is the whole strategy in one paragraph.

## The complaints, measured — this is the valuable part

One agent pulled **2,650 most-recent US App Store reviews** across the seven big farm/merge games and coded
the 741 that were 1-2 star and long enough to carry an argument. Another read the whole cozy-idle review set.
The result is not a mood, it is a ranking.

| # | The complaint | How common | The line that says it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **You can't get anywhere without paying** | 25% | *"Not worth it. Just a cash cow."* |
| 2 | **Energy ends the session in two minutes** | 20% (46% at Travel Town) | Two minutes to burn, three hours to refill |
| 3 | **The events never stop and can't be won** | 17% | Players setting **alarms through the night** to check in every 90 minutes |
| 4 | **The update took something away** | 13% (30% at Merge Mansion) | *"It used to be better"* |
| 5 | **The ad is not the game** | 13% (20% Gossip Harbor) | Review titled *"NOTHING LIKE THE ADS"* |
| = | **Bugs, lost progress, no support** | 13% (30% at Hay Day) | *"Lost all my data and have to restart the hole game"* |

**And then the finding that is worth more than all six.** Of **233 substantive five-star reviews** across
those same seven games, **exactly one** praises the game for being generous or fair.

> Nobody in this category is loved for how it treats you. They are loved for being relaxing, for the
> community, and for being a daily routine — never for generosity.

That is not a values position any more. **It is the only unoccupied square on the board, and it has been
counted.** Doc 17 files "no forced ads, no energy, no fake timers" under monetization; on this evidence it
belongs at the top of the positioning section with the numbers attached.

### Four more complaint patterns that are design instructions

**Ad *interruption*, not ad *presence*, is what kills a rating.** r/AndroidGaming's "fastest reason you have
uninstalled a mobile game?" thread: the top answer by a wide margin is *"Ads, it's always ads. If I've been hit
with ads multiple times before I start playing, the app gets deleted immediately."* The refined rule from the
same thread — **"I don't want ads I didn't explicitly trigger, and I don't want ads I'm not getting anything
for"** — is doc 37's placement policy, written by a player. Adorable Garden and Hatch Dragons have the same
1M+ install tier; the ad-free-by-default one holds 4.6-4.9 and the interstitial one holds 3.7.

**Upkeep that scales with the collection is the cozy-idle churn killer, and it is aimed straight at us.**
Window Garden's own reviews: *"you collect so many plants that you cant water properly"*, *"this game wants to be
a second job"*, *"The fact that the plants die makes this game lose the 'cozy' aspect."* Meanwhile a new
competitor, **Desktop Pet: Idle Cats**, launched on 2 Aug 2026 selling *the absence* of upkeep as its headline
feature — "no stressful survival meters, no cleaning chores, and no punishment for stepping away." The market
is actively correcting away from punishing maintenance. **This lands directly on our creature-food design and
it needs an answer, not a shrug** — see "where we disagree" below.

**Content exhaustion in days is the recurring 2025-26 failure, and it is the argument for the Turn.** Evergrove:
everything unlocked in 3 days. Terrarium: *"there's no way to beat it... now there's nothing to do but to regrow
the plants."* Pocket Plants: *"i currently have 1 billion energy. billion with a B, with nothing to spend it
on."* Rusty's Retirement's top negative review, 256 helpful: *"at only 22 hours into the game I had already
completed it"* and there is *"nothing incremental, no new game+ system, nothing at all you would expect in
current idle games."*

**Generative AI art is now a refund trigger in this exact audience.** r/incremental_games made AI disclosure
**mandatory on every developer post** on 2 June 2026 — you must include a section headed "AI Disclosure" even if
you used none, or the post is removed. r/CozyGamers runs a blocklist thread (1,769 upvotes) where players report
refunding on sight. Of the 100 most recent negative Steam reviews for 2026's cozy breakout *Librarian*, **73
mention AI**. Tiny Harvest and Evergrove are both being marked down for it right now. **A designer drawing every
flower by hand is a commercial asset in 2026, not just a nice thing.** Note the tolerated line, drawn by the
community itself: *"Generative AI was exclusively used as a coding assistant... no AI-generated text, art, or
audio assets"* (150 upvotes). Code help is fine and routinely disclosed. Art and text are not.

## The prestige question, answered

The brief asked directly: did anyone ship a cozy idle game with a prestige loop between Jan 2025 and Aug 2026?

**No.** We looked hard.

- Every larger cozy launch in the window — Hatch Dragons, Evergrove, Chillquarium, Cozy Cat Tree, Cats & Soup:
  Magic Recipe, Magic Garden — runs on **collection + events + gacha, with no prestige layer at all.**
- The one cozy botanical game with a prestige-shaped reset is **Bloom Idle**, a solo dev at 10K installs.
- The 2026 incremental scene's prestige games are all **uncozy** — mining, tower defence, space, civilisation.
  Not one is a garden.
- The only reset loop at scale in our lane is **Adorable Garden's forced per-location restart, which its players
  hate**, for a reason we can learn from: it is imposed rather than offered, and **nothing but numbers carries
  across**.
- And there is unprompted *demand*: the Window Garden five-star review above, asking for exactly this.

**Cozy-plus-prestige is an empty intersection, not a crowded one.** That is the opportunity and it is also the
risk: untested is not the same as proven. The honest framing for the owner is that **the Turn is the only thing
in this project nobody else has, and nobody else has tried it either.**

Three design constraints fall straight out of the evidence, and they are cheap insurance:

1. **The Turn must be offered, never imposed.** Doc 32 already has this right — invited, never forced, the word
   "reset" appears nowhere. Adorable Garden's one-star is what the other version feels like.
2. **The attachment has to travel.** Their player bonded with sunflowers that had faces and then lost them. Our
   creatures, the Almanac, the Century Bloom and every running long timer already survive the Turn. **Say so
   loudly, in the ceremony, in the store listing and in the clip.**
3. **The gate is the payoff.** Doc 32's "the reset pays in places first and numbers second" is, on this evidence,
   the single most defensible design call in the project.

## The positioning map

Two axes, both drawn from what actually separates these games in their reviews.

```
              HIGH CRAFT / MADE BY A HAND
                          ▲
       Florisium ●        │        ● Hatch Dragons
       Usagi Shima ●      │          (Runaway Play)
       Window Garden ●    │
       Bloom Idle ●       │        ● Cats & Soup
                          │
   ◄──────────────────────┼──────────────────────►
   GENEROUS               │              EXTRACTIVE
   (opt-in only,          │     (energy, forced ads,
    nothing is taken)     │      timed offers, gacha)
                          │
       Cozy Farm ●        │        ● Adorable Garden
       Tiny Harvest ●     │        ● The Cozy Florist
       (AI art)           │        ● Terrarium
                          ▼
              LOW CRAFT / MADE BY A MACHINE OR A TEMPLATE

     ★ GARDEN WONDER aims for the top-left — and the top-left is where
       everybody earns nothing. The Turn is what is supposed to fix that.
```

**Who is closest.** Three, in order:

1. **Window Garden.** Same shape, same size of team, ad-free-ish, 4.82 stars, shipping monthly, and its player
   has already written our feature request. If it ships a prestige loop we lose our only uncontested claim.
   **This is the one to watch.**
2. **Florisium.** Same *promise*, better art budget, an Apple feature already banked — but no economy and no
   loop, so it cannot answer "why is this run better than the last one." It has no runs.
3. **Runaway Play.** Not competing with us today. But they are ~30 people with a repeatable cozy-collection
   playbook, an Apple editorial relationship, and **a bee-and-flowers game already on the shelf**. If we chart,
   they can ship a response in a year.

### The three axes where we are genuinely alone

| # | The axis | Who else is on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A cozy game with a prestige loop.** A reset that is offered, ceremonial, and pays you in *new places* rather than a bigger multiplier. | **Nobody at any scale.** One solo dev at 10K installs. |
| 2 | **Generosity as the product, not the apology.** Opt-in-only ads, no energy, no loot boxes, nothing taken while you are away, every collectible earnable by play. | **Nobody.** One five-star review in 233 praises fairness. The claim itself is copyable (Tiny Harvest made it); what is not copyable is making it *true* while also earning. |
| 3 | **Mario-Wonder-grade juice on an idle garden.** Every tap answers, squash-and-stretch, overshoot easing, confetti reserved for milestones. | **Nobody in cozy botanical.** But see the warning below — this axis is narrowing. |

**The honest caveat on axis 3, and doc 17 should absorb it.** Doc 17 says the lane is "visually dominated by
muted pastels, lo-fi and pixel art" and that craft is therefore the two-person moat. That was true of the lane
doc 17 surveyed. The two most *editorially successful* botanical games of the window are neither: Florisium is a
509MB naturalistic 3D flower sim with HDR capture, Hatch Dragons is 656MB of painterly 3D, and **both won Apple
"GAMES WE LOVE" slots**. The differentiation axis in the featured tier is shifting toward production values,
which is the one axis where two people are structurally disadvantaged. The conclusion survives — craft is still
the moat — but the premise ("the lane is visually uniform, so standing out is easy") is weakening. Our craft
argument has to be about *feel*, which is cheap, rather than *fidelity*, which is not.

## What a player is actually worth — the TAPCLAP case

*Added 2026-08-30, from an owner question about a studio whose HTML5 games have high play counts on
CrazyGames. It turned out to be the best-evidenced version of an argument this document was already making
with a four-year-old number.*

**TAPCLAP is not an HTML5 indie.** It is a fifteen-year-old Cyprus studio — founded 2010 as Orange Apps,
rebranded 2017, self-funded with no outside investment — making mobile match-3 and merge games that *also* run
in a browser. The HTML5 part is a distribution surface, not the product.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Google Play, all seven apps | **~80 million lifetime installs, ~1M ratings** |
| Pirate Treasures (2015) | **62 million installs** |
| Candy Valley (2016) | 8.8M |
| DesignVille (2021) · Cross Stitch Masters (2020) | 5M+ each |
| Mansion Tale (Apr 2025) · TropicVille (2026) | 100K+ · 5K+ |
| **US App Store ratings, whole portfolio, lifetime** | **~15,000** |
| Headcount | **"More than 70" (2020) → about 30 (July 2024)** |

Read the last two rows together. **A game with 62 million installs has 1,149 lifetime US iOS ratings.** That
audience is Russia/CIS and emerging markets — historically VK and OK.ru, where Pirate Treasures had 1.55M daily
users in 2020 — and it monetises at a fraction of tier-1. And the studio has more than halved.

**JUDGMENT — the revenue estimate, and how it was reached.** No reliable figure is publicly retrievable; the
cleanest datapoint found puts Pirate Treasures at roughly **$20K/month in early 2023**, and Sensor Tower's
public per-country pages are too inconsistent to lean on (one shows the game peaking at **$43** in Georgia,
below several games nobody has heard of). **So the estimate is built from headcount, which for a self-funded
studio is the honest proxy:** thirty people in Limassol is roughly $1.5-2.5M a year in payroll, they are
covering it, and they have been going fifteen years. That puts them at **roughly $2-4M a year across IAP, ads,
browser portals and any licensing** — call it $150-350K a month, mostly ads, mostly not from the App Store.
Trending down, on the headcount evidence.

### The comparison that makes the point

Lifetime revenue per install, across three storefronts. **All three are estimates and the TAPCLAP one is the
softest** — but the spread is an order of magnitude, so the ranking survives any reasonable error.

| Game | Reach | Revenue | **Per install** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **TAPCLAP portfolio** | ~80M installs over 15 years | ~$20M cumulative *(judgment)* | **~$0.25** |
| **The Cozy Florist** | 7M installs since Dec 2025 | $6M (AppMagic) | **~$0.86** |
| **Rusty's Retirement** | 330,000 units in four months | ~$1.6M gross at $7 | **~$5** |

> **Eighty million installs supports thirty people. It used to support seventy.**

That is the Terrarium argument — enormous reach, no reason to pay — with a fifteen-year track record behind it
instead of a stale October-2022 estimate. **Where this doc previously leaned on Terrarium's unverifiable
"$9K/month", lean on this instead.**

### Two things it settles for us

**It supports the web-funnel ruling in [39-growth-and-launch.md](39-growth-and-launch.md), from the strongest
possible source.** TAPCLAP's CrazyGames pages carry App Store and Play Store links out to **their own**
listings — not, as with the games CrazyGames itself ports, to a `com.CrazyGames.*` bundle. A studio that has
been doing this for a decade uses the portal as a shop window pointing at mobile, and takes the money
elsewhere. That is exactly the shape doc 39 recommends.

**And it sharpens the premium-PC question.** The three rows above are the same kind of content on three
storefronts, and the per-install spread is twenty-fold. It is one more reason the "$3,650 lifetime per idle
title" graveyard should be read as a *mobile free-to-play* statistic rather than a fact about idle games.

**Not verified, recorded as a gap:** the CrazyGames play counts that prompted the question. Neither the Mansion
Tale nor the DesignVille game page displayed a plays figure on fetch. If the browser traffic really is large
while the revenue is this thin, that is a sharper version of the same lesson and worth a second look.

## Five more things worth knowing

**The lane is growing while the market shrinks.** Farming did **$576M in H1 2026, +15% YoY, on 172M downloads,
+33%** — [confirmed at source](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-casual-games-in-h1), with AppMagic
stating the genre is "not yet saturated". Meanwhile total mobile games IAP was **$40bn, down 2%**, on downloads
**down 12%**. And casual downloads have fallen from **11.2bn in H1 2021 to 6.34bn in H1 2026 — a 43% collapse.**
Read that together: more money in our lane, fewer people walking past the shop. **The funnel is the problem, not
the market.** Also note who takes the money: **Hay Day alone is 13% of that $576M** and set a **$1m single-day
record on 1 May 2026**, thirteen years after launch, with no energy bar.

**The graveyard statistic is a *mobile free-to-play* statistic.** Doc 29's whole framing rests on "$3,650
lifetime per idle title". In the *desktop-companion* lane next door, a first-time solo developer took ~$1.6M
gross in four months (Rusty's Retirement) and a specialist incremental publisher just entered the category.
Same content, different storefront, completely different revenue distribution. That is an argument for taking a
premium PC build seriously as a *revenue line*, not only as a funnel — and it is the strongest thing the critic
found.

**Netflix is out of cozy, and doc 29's ceiling argument leans on it.** Doc 29 caps direction A with "Cats &
Soup's $300K/month comes with Netflix distribution attached." Netflix **sold Spry Fox back to its founders in
December 2025**, closed Boss Fight in October 2025, and closed Night School Studio and Moonloot on **13 August
2026 — sixteen days ago**. Separately, the Cats & Soup Netflix Edition is frozen: last real build October 2024,
3.30 stars from 172 ratings. **Cats & Soup did not win because of Netflix.** Strike "get a Netflix deal" from the
mental list of exits.

**The mobile clone tier failed.** Doc 17 warned the Grow a Garden gap was closing. It has not closed — the
biggest clone renamed itself away from the keyword, two others are delisted, and the survivor sits at 1.7M
Android installs, still on version 0.9.2, being one-starred because its paid ad-removal doesn't work. **The real
competition arrived from funded cozy studios instead.** There is also a large locked-out audience with no
legitimate product: clone reviewers repeatedly say *"I am not aloud to have rob lox so I got this"*, *"its banned
from my country"*. That audience is younger than the 16-24 skew doc 17 records for Roblox, which has real
consequences for our age rating (see [40-financial-model.md](40-financial-model.md)).

**Discord Activities are a channel nobody in our docs has named.** Magic Garden — claimed 2M+ players — ran
Discord-first and used iOS-exclusive plants to pull people into the app. Discord Activities are web apps in an
iframe, which is exactly what Garden Wonder already is. It is also **the only surface where a shared sky could be
*demonstrated* rather than asserted**: everyone in a voice channel is in the same session at the same wall-clock
minute. Worth an evening to investigate; not worth betting on until someone checks the approval process and
whether discovery is real.

## Where this document disagrees with the standing docs

Recorded plainly, because the docs-discipline rule says a refresh that quietly contradicts is worse than useless.

1. **Doc 17's retention table is aspirational, not baseline.** Fix it with both rows: our target *and* the 2026
   all-genre distribution, and stress-test every plan against the median. This is the correction that matters
   most.
2. **Doc 17 should stop calling Cats & Soup an indie comparable** and should stop citing Egg Inc's "generosity"

3. **Doc 17 has no competitor entry for Runaway Play.** Honey Grove appears only as a visual reference. They are

4. **"No forced ads, no energy, no fake timers" should be promoted** from a monetization note to the headline
   positioning claim, with the 1-in-233 number attached.
5. **The punishing-upkeep direction is the one place where the evidence runs against the owner's stated taste,
   and this document will not soften it.** Window Garden loses stars exactly where upkeep scales with the
   collection; Tiny Pasture's top negative review reads *"Listing it as an idler, but punishing you for being

## Confidence notes — read before quoting anything here

- **Reddit was blocked to most agents.** The community findings come from the Arctic Shift public archive and
  from store review feeds. Permalinks are correct; the pages themselves were mostly not fetched directly.
- **Store review feeds skew negative.** These games hold 4.3-4.8 aggregate ratings while 37-60% of their most
  recent reviews are 1-2 star. The complaint percentages are **shares of people who bothered to complain**, not
  shares of players. They tell you what the unhappy few are unhappy about, which is what marketing copy needs.
- **Sensor Tower, AppMagic and data.ai are one company.** Treat their agreement as one vote.
- **Tenjin publishes no 2026 eCPM data** — its only country-level rewarded charts are labelled Q1/Q2 **2024**.
  Any "2026 Tenjin rewarded eCPM" figure in circulation is a mislabelled two-year-old number.
- **A wave of AI-generated statistics farms now dominates benchmark search results** — gameinsights.ai,
  playio.co, progamespoint, zipdo, worldmetrics and friends. One of them claims casual D1 of 38% and LTV of $45,
  which no credible dataset supports. **Rule: if a figure's only home is a domain nobody in the industry has
  heard of, it does not go in a doc.**
- **Not checked, and stated as gaps:** subreddit and Discord sentiment for Cats & Soup and Terrarium; Chillquarium's
  mobile reviews (the most relevant port case we have); any Android install figure; and the Grow a Garden owner's
  litigation posture, where the delistings are consistent with pressure but no filing was found.
