The Garden Year

You don't walk a map. You swipe through the year — and turning it is the prestige.

Your seasonal-gardens idea doesn't just work — it's the missing half of the prestige design. Prestige systems live or die on one feeling: what did the reset actually give me? Numbers-go-up is the genre's answer. Yours is better: the reset gives you a place. You turn the year, and the year has somewhere new in it.

Prestige pays in placesThe scariest thing about a reset is losing your world. Here, the first reset opens Fall. Nobody uninstalls over that.
Four speeds, one gameSeconds, hours, days, and the meta — the snacker and the twice-a-day player are both fully fed.
A sentence nobody owns"Swipe through the seasons of your garden." That's the store line, and it's a five-second clip.

The world is one swipe wide

Horizontal moves you through time. Vertical stays what it already is — altitude, down to the Hollow. No map screen, no menu: the year is the geography.

Four seasons, four speeds

Each garden is a different clock and a different rule — that's what keeps four boards from being four clones.

Summer seconds

The garden you already built. Tapping, combos, procs, fast seeds — the fidget toy, the 7-minute sit-down. Nothing about it changes; it just stops carrying the whole game alone.

Home. Open from the first tap.

Fall hours

The harvest garden. Plant a whole bed over coffee, come back at dinner to a windfall — everything ripens in waves, and one big pop beats forty small ones. Orchard trees, pumpkins, the cottage crops live here.

Opens at turn 1 — the first prestige reward is a world.

Winter days

The night shift. Day-long clocks under snow, huge payouts — and the twist: Winter grows at full speed while you're away. It's the garden that works while you sleep. Tuck it in at night, wake up to a payout.

Opens around turn 3, when the player's rhythm exists.

Spring the meta

The nursery. Saved seeds get planted here, new flower lines are bred here, and one day creatures hatch here. Spring is the garden about the other three — where the long game lives.

Opens around turn 6. The last gate, and the deepest.
The one rule

A season is a speed and a rule — never a re-skin.

If Fall is just orange Summer, we've built four clones of one garden, and that's the exact decay pattern the research keeps warning about. Different clock, different verb, same board grammar — the meadow already proved a re-skinned board with a new rule feels like a new place.

The Turn — what prestiging actually is

About a minute of ceremony, one choice, one gate. Here's the whole ride, start to finish.

Saved Seeds — the currency

One pouch, filled at the turn. Three rules keep it feeling fair, and each one exists because a famous idle game got it wrong.

Earned for the year you grewThe count comes from everything you harvested and earned all year — not what's sitting in your wallet at the end.
Spending gold never costs youBuy every badge, every plot, every seed — your pouch doesn't care. There is no "should I stop buying things before I turn?" Ever.
Every year pays a little moreVeteran years are worth more seeds, so the pouch keeps up as your ambitions (and walls) get bigger.

Why not leftover gold, since that was the first instinct? Because if seeds came from unspent gold, the smart move would be to stop playing near the end of every year — no planting, no upgrades, just hoarding until the turn. The game would quietly punish you for playing it. Counting the whole year instead means the best way to earn seeds is always just… gardening harder.

🪙 Gold — the year's money
  • Earned all year, by everything
  • Buys badges, plots, plants, seed unlocks
  • Resets at the turn — after the pouch is minted
Spend it like it's going out of style. It is.
🌰 Saved Seeds — the forever money
  • Minted once, at the turn, from the whole year
  • Buys petals — and only petals
  • Never resets, never expires
The only thing it can't buy is anything temporary.

Because neither wallet can buy the other's things, no purchase ever competes with your future. That single wall is why every prestige game you've loved invented a second currency — heavenly chips, angels, soul eggs — instead of letting gold do both jobs.

Flower mastery — where the seeds go

Every flower in the Almanac gets its own little card, and Saved Seeds buy petals on it. This is the "level up each flower" system — here's what the Daisy's card looks like.

Two shared skills, everywhereRich Bloom and Quick Sprout on every flower. Boring on purpose — they're the "can't go wrong" petals, and they're why there is no wrong way to spend.
One signature skill eachThe Daisy drops card packs. The Moonflower could court storms (mutations). The Bluebell could call its creature sooner. The signature is where a flower's personality lives — and we only need to write five or six for launch.
Chances are countdownsNever "+0.4% chance" — always "every 12th → every 10th," with the counter visible on the card. You bought a thing you can watch coming.
Everything maxes, eventuallyIt's a garden to fill, not an exam to pass. No petal is a mistake; a completionist can genuinely finish a flower's card. (Gems stay global — no flower becomes "the gem farm.")

How it's introduced — one petal at a time

The system never lands as a wall of trees. It arrives in four beats, and the player is never asked to understand more than one new thing per turn.

Year one: nothingNo mastery UI exists. You just play the garden you have, and a little year-meter quietly fills. The mystery IS the tutorial.
Turn one: one choiceThe full ceremony, and exactly one decision — bless a flower. That blessing is your first petal, free. Gate opens, Fall exists, done. No shop, no tree, no reading.
The morning afterA small pouch of Saved Seeds appears, and the Almanac's flower rows now show petals. You spend your first few on whatever you like — there are only two skills per flower to even look at.
Turn two onward: the ritualTurn → pouch fills → petals → replant. Signature skills reveal themselves as flowers earn their first petals, so the catalog deepens exactly as fast as the player does.
Your badges don't move. Power Punch, Quick Grip, Lucky Charm, Star Strike, Combo Coil — all stay exactly what they are: the inside-the-year game, bought with gold, reset with the year, rebought stronger and faster each spring. The rebuild morning is half the fun of a fresh year (it's the same ritual AdCap players love about re-hiring managers). The Harvest Drone and per-plot harvesters are deliberately not in any of this yet — that's our separate conversation, and nothing here depends on how it lands.

Two weeks in the life

One player, gamer's-eye view. The colors on the rail show which garden owns that day.

Day 1

The toy

You tap a flower and it talks back. Coins arc, daisies go in, the first upgrades land. Twenty minutes later you open the seed book and the third flower costs six times everything you've earned tonight. And when you swipe right, you bump a hedge gate with orange leaves drifting behind it. Locked.

Feels like: charmed, fidgety, one eyebrow up at that gate.
Day 2

The wall gets personal

Daily done, quests cleared, tap upgrades bought — your number is visibly moving, but the wall isn't. Meanwhile a little meter up top has been quietly filling all along. It says autumn is coming. Wait. Is the wall… the point?

Feels like: plotting.
Day 3

The first turn

The meter fills at the wall and the flower asks: "The year's turning. Save your seeds?" The garden goes gold, petals everywhere, and you pick one flower to bless. Your coins become Saved Seeds. And then the hedge gate opens — FALL is a whole second garden, with plants that take hours and payouts that make Summer look cute.

Feels like: "oh — it's THAT kind of game." This is the screenshot.
Days 4–5

The rhythm sets in

Plant Fall over coffee. Tap Summer while you wait. Harvest the windfall at dinner. The second turn comes the same evening — the reclimb takes an afternoon, not two days, because your blessed daisies grow fat and fast. Now you're routing: bless the tulip next, or bank for the new seed? Even snack sessions have a finish line: 3 more harvests to a gem.

Feels like: a routine you chose, not one assigned to you.
Days 6–7

Winter opens

Turn three lifts the frost gate. Day-long clocks under snow — and the trick lands the first morning: Winter grew all night while the app was closed. Sunday evening you notice you have three rituals now, and you built them yourself.

Feels like: the game respects your sleep. Cosy players tell friends about exactly this.
Week 2

Spring, and the long game

Around turn six the last gate opens. Spring is the nursery: your saved seeds go in the ground here, new lines get bred, and the walls between seeds are days long now — and that feels fair, because four gardens run at four speeds and something is always about to pop. You plant your first Century Bloom: fourteen days to flower. You check it like a tamagotchi. You will absolutely post it when it opens.

Feels like: tenure. The thing idle games are actually for.
The morning check40 seconds. Collect Winter, glance at the meter, go to work.
The lunch flip2 minutes. Harvest Fall's windfall, replant the beds.
The evening sit-down7 minutes. Tap, route, bless, turn the year when it's ripe.

Four players, one game

The real payoff of four clocks: four completely different people are all fully fed — by the same build.

The Snacker

lives in summer

Five three-minute pops a day. Combos, procs, pity counters. Never reads a number bigger than the screen. The tap loop was built for this person and it stays theirs.

The Scheduler

lives in fall & winter

Coffee and bedtime, two visits, barely taps. Plants long clocks and loves the welcome-back scene. Today's game gives this person almost nothing. The Garden Year gives them half the world.

The Optimizer

lives in spring

Routes blessings, times turns, keeps a spreadsheet nobody asked for. This is the r/incremental_games crowd — the one channel that has launched games like ours for free.

The Collector

lives everywhere

Fills almanac rows, chases jars, creatures, cards. Turns the year when the game invites it, not when the math says. The biggest slice of our actual audience.

The field guide — every word, plainly

The one-line version of everything on this page. When any conversation gets too technical, these are the words.

The YearOne playthrough of your garden, spring to the Turn.
The TurnThe prestige moment: end the year, cash it in, start fresh with permanent bonuses. Never called a reset.
GoldThe year's money. Spend it freely — it goes away at the Turn.
Saved SeedsThe forever money. You only get them at the Turn; they buy petals; you never lose them.
The wellEverything you've ever earned fills one deep well of Saved Seeds. Each Turn scoops out what's new. Turn often = small scoops, wait = one big scoop — same total either way, so there's no wrong rhythm.
The meterFills as your year grows, pulses when a Turn is ready. It lives in the dock's Turn button.
The TallyThe arcade scoreboard at the Turn — bonus lines for orders filled, windfalls, species grown — multiplies your scoop, up to double. A line you scored zero on never appears.
The blessingThe one flower you pick at each Turn. It gets one free petal — a permanent upgrade you'd normally pay Saved Seeds for, handed to the flower you choose. The ceremony's only decision: who had the best year?
PetalsPermanent upgrades on each flower: worth more, grows faster, plus one special skill each. Bought with Saved Seeds.
Unlock priceThe one-time gold price to open a new seed. Paid once, yours forever — it survives every Turn.
The seasonsGardens at different speeds. Summer is seconds, Fall is hours, Winter is days, Spring is the long game.
The windfallFall's bonus for harvesting the whole bed at once — the dinner appointment.
The Century BloomThe fourteen-day showpiece plant. Survives every Turn.
SignaturesEach flower's one special petal skill — the Daisy drops card packs, the Moonflower courts storms. A later phase.

Decided here, still open there

Where the brainstorm stands after this round.

  • DecidedThe turn clears fast annuals in the main garden only. A running long timer — Winter's sleepers, a mid-grow Century Bloom — always survives the year turning. A reset that eats a two-week plant is the one unforgivable version of this.
  • DecidedThe year is the player's own calendar. The meter invites the turn; nothing forces it. Real-time shared weather can still play on top of every season later — player-owned prestige, world-owned sky.
  • DecidedArt starts as a background swap per season. Same board, four backdrops. We lean into real per-season art once the feel is proven — the same MVP-first call that built the map.
  • OpenThe Harvest Drone and per-plot harvesters. Parked for their own conversation. Nothing in the mastery system depends on where they land.
  • OpenDo Fall and Winter beds ever clear? Lean: never automatically — the turn is a main-garden event, and the other seasons keep their own rhythm. Worth one playtest before locking.
Brainstorm only — nothing here touches the repo or the live game. Say go and this becomes a numbered doc with the prestige spec folded in; say no and it cost us an evening.
